# Process

I used Grok when writing this essay.

**Drafting**  
I first wrote a draft that included my experiences with AI image generation and oblique photography in the digital-twin project, as well as the core idea I wanted to explore: “describing is not the same as specifying.” I then asked Grok to polish the language and check the word count. I retained my original expression for several key sentences that felt personal.

**One thing I kept**  
Grok suggested the precise wording: “the difference between being a passenger and being a pilot of the same system.” This metaphor is clearer and more concise than my original longer version. It accurately captures the distinction I wanted to make about how different people use the same tools. I kept it because it aligns with my real experience using those machines.

**One thing I rejected**  
Grok repeatedly offered a polished but generic paragraph arguing that “learning programming teaches computational thinking that remains valuable even when code is generated automatically.” Although the statement is technically true, it was too abstract and safe. I deleted it for it could have been written by anyone and had no concrete connection to my own practice.

I also asked Grok for help with APA citation formatting, and then verified each reference myself.

The final structure, tone, and decisions about what to keep or cut were made by me.
